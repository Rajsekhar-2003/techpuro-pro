Generative AI refers to machine learning systems that **create new content** instead of only analysing existing data. Examples include chat assistants, image generators, code assistants and tools that synthesise speech or video.

## How generative AI works

A model is trained on a very large dataset and adjusts its internal parameters to capture patterns in that data. When given a prompt, it produces output that is statistically consistent with what it learned.

### Large language models

Large language models (LLMs) are usually built on the **transformer** architecture. They break text into tokens and predict the next token repeatedly to form sentences and paragraphs.

### Diffusion models

Many image generators use **diffusion**. They learn to remove noise from images step by step, and generate a new image by starting from noise and refining it towards a prompt.

## Common types of generative AI

| Type | Typical output | Common technique |
|------|----------------|------------------|
| Text | Articles, summaries, answers | Transformer LLMs |
| Image | Illustrations, photo-style images | Diffusion models |
| Code | Functions, tests, explanations | Code-trained LLMs |
| Audio | Speech, music | Neural audio models |

## Practical uses

- Drafting and summarising documents
- Assisting developers with code and debugging
- Creating design concepts and marketing imagery
- Translating and tutoring

## Limitations and risks

- **Hallucinations:** confident but false output.
- **Bias:** models reflect biases present in training data.
- **Privacy and copyright:** training data and generated content raise legal and ethical questions that are still evolving.

## Conclusion

Generative AI is a powerful productivity tool when used with human review. Understanding how it works helps you choose the right tool and avoid over-trusting its output.
